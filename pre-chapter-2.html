Pre-Chapter 2 — “Ce mai faceți?”

Short “legend” page: how to greet, describe people with a fi, use un/o, demonstratives, time words, and three key verbs: a merge, a face, a cere.

Go to practice (Post-Chapter 2)
1) Greetings + “How are you?” (tu / dumneata / dumneavoastră)
Ce mai faci? — How are you? (informal, to a friend)
Ce mai faceți? — How are you? (polite / to adults / public)
Bine, mulțumesc. — Fine, thanks.
Nu prea bine. — Not too well.
Safe rule: if unsure, use dumneavoastră + plural verb form (e.g., faceți).
2) Adjectives with “a fi” (agreement m/f)
Sorin e drăguț. — Sorin is nice.
Sorin e timid. — Sorin is shy.
Sorin e interesant. — Sorin is interesting.
Corina e drăguță. — Corina is nice.
Corina e timidă. — Corina is shy.
Corina e interesantă. — Corina is interesting.
Many adjectives add -ă for feminine: drăguț → drăguță, timid → timidă.
3) Frequency: always / sometimes / never
întotdeauna — always
uneori — sometimes
nu… niciodată — never (you need nu + niciodată)
Example: (Eu) nu sînt niciodată obosit. — I am never tired.
4) Indefinite article “un / o” + the key rule (with adjectives)
Rule from your pages: nationality/occupation usually has no article after a fi, BUT if it has an adjective, you use un/o.
Sorin e inginer. — Sorin is an engineer.
Maria e reporteriță. — Maria is a reporter.
Sorin e un inginer bun. — Sorin is a good engineer.
Maria e o reporteriță bună. — Maria is a good reporter.
5) Demonstratives: acesta / aceasta / acela / aceea
Masculine / NeuterFeminineMeaning
acestaaceastathis (one)
acelaaceeathat (one)
If you don’t know the gender yet, the book suggests using the feminine form to ask: Ce este aceasta/aceea?
6) Time words + sequence (first… then… after…)
acum — now
astăzi — today
astă-seară — this evening
mîine — tomorrow
după masă — in the afternoon / after lunch
mai întîi — first
apoi — then / after
după + (noun) — afterward / after + noun
Example: După masă merg la bibliotecă. — After lunch I go to the library.
7) Three key verbs (present): a face / a merge / a cere (+ a crede)
a face can mean “to do/make”. With mai → it becomes the idiom “how are you?”: Ce mai faci?
Eu fac. — I do/make
Tu faci. — you do
El/Ea face. — he/she does
Eu merg. — I go
Tu mergi. — you go
Noi mergem. — we go
Eu cer un ceai. — I’m asking for a tea.
Eu nu cer nimic. — I’m not asking for anything.
a crede (to believe/think):
cred, crezi, crede, credem, credeți, cred
8) “A merge în” vs “A merge la” (destinations)
a merge în Europa / în România / în Transilvania — go to (continent/country/region)
a merge la București / la Sibiu / la o cafenea — go to (city / specific place)
Fast memory: în = big areas (country/region), la = cities/places.
9) Pronunciation (optional, for English speakers): ce/ci and ge/gi
From your page: when c is followed by e/i, it sounds like “ch” in English; when g is followed by e/i, it sounds like “j”.
ce, cine, cer, face
Try: Ce mai faceți?
general, geolog, gimnastică, inginer, merge
Try: Lucia cere un ceai.
